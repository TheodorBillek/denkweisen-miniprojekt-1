Cheat Sheet: Names & Greetings in Forms

Best practices for developers.

The five most important things from what we read

  1. Everyone has a given name and a family name: We will have a legal name field for identification, and a second field for the given name or the name they want to be addressed by. The email will use the second field. This way we can check identity and still let people use the name they prefer.
  2. Names only use plain Latin letters: It should be obvious why this one is included. see form 4
  3. Names have a maximum length: The same applies here.
  4. Gender can be read from a name or fixed to two options: It can't be inferred, so let people specify it or leave it out. see form 3
  5. The legal name is the name to greet someone with: We greet people the way they want to be greeted. The legal name is only for identification.

Names

  • Don't split or modify names if you somehow can. We never assume that we can split a name, or that people have 1, 2 or any specific amount of names. see form 5
  • Support as many characters as you can. We are in a modern day and age, we have the technological capability and we have the storage available to us. see form 4

Legal name & how people want to be addressed

  • If you need some kind of identifier for in-person authentication or so, you can ask for a legal name and then just expect them to show an ID with that name that matches them.
  • But it's also a good idea to include an option to add an alternative way of being called. Having it done with a tick box is a good idea, because then it doesn't disturb people who don't need it, but people who want it can use it. Best for both and little effort for us.
  • I mean, a lot of sites identify you with an email address and have you choose a username. This isn't that different.
  • And for your form of address, have a couple of options including a none, or just let people make their own.

Data

  • Store things in separate data entries (note: have backups). If you chuck it all into one column, it makes personalised greetings hell. see form 2
  • Check for exactly what the field sends: if you check for "herr" while the field is "Herr", it still won't pick the right one, and you end up addressing everyone with "Frau". see form 3

Security

  • Make sure your form/site doesn't include any of the OWASP Top 10. They are easy to check, and you can even hire people to do this for relatively cheap, and it will stop a lot of attacks. One presented here was SQL injection. see form 1
